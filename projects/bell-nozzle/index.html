Optimized Bell Nozzle

A Mach 3.5 LOX/RP-1 nozzle, sized from NASA CEA data for a 100 bar chamber and a 0.7 bar exit, drawn as a parametric shell, meshed for a compressible solver, and printed.

Type
Design and analysis
Propellants
LOX / RP-1, O/F 2.56
Geometry
5.0 mm throat, 18.55 mm exit
Tools
NASA CEA, OpenSCAD, OpenFOAM, FDM

Sizing

The target was an exit Mach number of 3.5 at roughly 3 km altitude, from a 100 bar chamber. NASA CEA gave the gas properties for the LOX/RP-1 pair at an O/F ratio of 2.56, which set the ratio of specific heats and the molecular weight the rest of the sizing depends on.

Those numbers fix the area ratio, and the area ratio fixes the geometry: a 5.0 mm throat radius and an 18.55 mm exit radius, an area ratio of 13.8, over a 45 mm bell.

Simulation

The geometry went into a compressible steady-state solver on a hex-dominant mesh. It did not converge. Residuals stalled and the run diverged around iteration 24, with a pressure ratio of roughly 143 to 1 and a 3615 K inlet driving gradients the steady solver could not hold.

Printing it

The nozzle was modeled in OpenSCAD as a parametric shell with a 2.0 mm wall and printed on an FDM machine. Printing it upside down — widest end flat on the bed — lets the parabolic wall support itself, so no internal support material touches the expansion curve.
