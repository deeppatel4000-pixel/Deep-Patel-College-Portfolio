Mach 3.5 LOX/RP-1 Bell Nozzle Design and Analysis

This project follows the full engineering cycle of a high-pressure bell nozzle, from thermodynamic sizing to simulation-backed validation. I used NASA CEA data to size the geometry for a 100 bar combustion system and then translated those targets into a CAD and CFD workflow focused on exit performance at a 3 km operating altitude.

The work pushed beyond a simple render by testing solver stability, interpreting residual behavior, and thinking carefully about manufacturability for a physical print. It became a great example of how aerospace design is really an iterative loop between theory, software, and practical constraints.

Engineering Goal

Achieve a theoretical exit Mach of 3.5 with a geometry optimized for high-pressure supersonic expansion.

Key Result

Predicted exit velocity of 2450 m/s and a verified supersonic core before solver limits were reached.

Modeling

NASA CEA sizing translated into CAD geometry and a custom bell profile.

Validation

rhoSimpleFoam CFD with a hex-dominant mesh and local throat refinement.

Fabrication

Designed for additive manufacturing with an inverted print orientation.

Case Study

Optimized Bell Nozzle

Mission Objective

Design a LOX/RP-1 rocket nozzle sized for a 100 bar combustion system and an exit Mach number of 3.5 at 3 km altitude.

Engineering Takeaway

Even when full steady-state convergence is difficult, simulation behavior can still reveal useful physical insight and guide smarter iteration.

Propellant Pair

LOX / RP-1

Exit Pressure Target

0.7 bar

Gas Properties

gamma = 1.2

Molecular Weight

Solver

Prototype Method

FDM 3D Printing

Project Summary

This project covered the full lifecycle of a propulsion concept: theoretical sizing from NASA CEA, geometry definition for a bell nozzle, CFD validation, and a manufacturability mindset for a physical print. The design targeted a high expansion case, which created meaningful solver challenges and made the analysis itself part of the engineering story.

The computational setup used a hex-dominant mesh with local throat refinement. Residual plots showed stabilized velocity and pressure behavior while enthalpy divergence appeared around iteration 24, highlighting the extreme numerical gradients inside the supersonic regime.

Engineering Workflow

1. Performance Targeting

Used NASA CEA output for a 100 bar combustion case and a 0.7 bar exit target at 3 km altitude.

2. Geometry Definition

Sized the throat, exit, and bell length to create a compact, high-expansion nozzle profile.

3. CFD Investigation

Meshed the computational domain with local throat refinement and analyzed residual behavior.

4. Manufacturing Strategy

Planned an inverted print orientation to preserve the internal curve without sacrificial support.

What I Learned

Theory to Geometry

Converting performance targets into throat, exit, and bell dimensions sharpened my understanding of nozzle sizing.

Solver Awareness

Residual behavior taught me how to read convergence critically instead of treating CFD as a black box.

Design for Buildability

The project reinforced that geometry quality and manufacturing strategy matter just as much as the ideal theoretical profile.
