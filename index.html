01

Software · Aerospace web application · 2026

ORBIX

ORBIX connects real aircraft and launch vehicles to the orbital mechanics, entry physics, and thermal analysis behind them, and every number stays attached to the source it came from. There are eleven surfaces in it: five aircraft, five launch vehicles, a comparison engine, an engineering lab with 33 modules, mission control and replay, a showcase, and a learning section.

02

Engineering · Self-directed propulsion project · April 2026

Bell Nozzle

I wanted to know how bell nozzles actually work, so I tried to design one. I drew the profile in OpenSCAD and revolved it into a shell, ran the flow volume through SimFlow and OpenFOAM, and printed the part in PLA. The simulation never converged, so most of the project turned into figuring out why.

The first cases died almost immediately. The best one reached iteration 24, then diverged.

OpenSCAD · SimFlow · OpenFOAM · ParaView · FDM

About

The part of engineering I like is the point where a design has to become a real object. Sizing a nozzle is arithmetic right up until you have to pick a wall thickness, decide which way up to print it, and work out what to do when the residuals stop falling.

I like software just as much. ORBIX started because I wanted to compare two launch vehicles properly and couldn't find anywhere that kept the units, the configuration, and the source attached to the numbers.
