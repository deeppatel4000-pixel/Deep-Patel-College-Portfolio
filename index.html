ORBIX

ORBIX connects landmark aircraft and launch vehicles to the orbital mechanics, entry physics, and thermal analysis behind them, with every value traced back to a published source. Five aircraft, five launch vehicles, a comparison engine, an engineering laboratory of 33 modules, mission control and replay, a showcase, and a learning section — eleven surfaces in all.

02

Engineering · Design and analysis · April 2026

Optimized Bell Nozzle

A Mach 3.5 LOX/RP-1 nozzle for a 100 bar chamber, sized from NASA CEA data, written as an OpenSCAD parametric shell, meshed and run in OpenFOAM, then printed inverted so the parabolic wall supports itself. The solve diverged at iteration 24. Working out why is the part I would defend.

Area ratio 13.8. Isentropic theory for Mach 3.5 at γ = 1.2 wants 13.77.

Propellants

About

The part of engineering I like is where an idea stops being a sketch. Sizing a nozzle is arithmetic until you have to pick a wall thickness, choose a print orientation that will not scar the expansion curve, and decide what to do when the residuals stop falling.

I am just as happy in software. ORBIX started because I wanted to compare two launch vehicles properly and could not find anywhere that kept the units, the configuration, and the source attached to the numbers.

Away from a screen: high jump and hurdles for Chelmsford Track and Field, VEX robotics, and volunteering with BAPS since 2017 — including three months of full-time fabrication work cutting architectural components on CNC and hot-wire machines.
