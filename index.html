Featured Project

Optimized Bell Nozzle

This is the main build on the site: a Mach 3.5 LOX/RP-1 rocket nozzle study using NASA CEA, CAD, CFD, and a design-for-manufacturing mindset. It is technical, but the goal is simple: make the design decisions clear.

CFD View
CAD Fusion 360 NASA CEA SimScale CFD

From equations to a nozzle shape I could actually test.

I sized the nozzle around a 100 bar LOX/RP-1 combustion case, then used simulation to see how the design behaved under a steep pressure drop. The CFD did not behave perfectly, and that honestly made the project more useful because it forced me to understand the solver, not just the final image.

Target

Mach 3.5 at 3 km altitude

Prediction

2450 m/s exit velocity

Geometry

5.0 mm throat, 18.55 mm exit

Lesson

Solver stability matters

Case Study

Optimized Bell Nozzle

Goal

Design a LOX/RP-1 rocket nozzle sized for a 100 bar combustion system and a Mach 3.5 exit target.

Takeaway

CFD is only useful when you understand the setup, the residuals, and the limits.

Propellant Pair

LOX / RP-1

Exit Pressure Target

0.7 bar

Solver

rhoSimpleFoam

Prototype Method

FDM 3D Printing

What happened

The simulation confirmed a supersonic core, but steady-state convergence hit numerical limits around iteration 24 because of the extreme pressure and temperature gradients. That became one of the most important lessons of the whole project.
